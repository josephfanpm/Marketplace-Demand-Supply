      Where posted hours go

      Every posted slot ends in one outcome, recorded in the slot outcomes table. These are measured, not estimated. The chip shows which team acts on it.

      Nobody appliedClient success · Recruiting

      No nurse applied to the slot.

      Whether it's the shift or the market: compare the market's fill with its expected fill.

      Applied, not bookedClient success

      Nurses applied, but the facility declined them or never confirmed, or the nurse withdrew.

      Deleted <24h before startClient success

      The facility deleted the slot less than 24 hours before the start, often because nobody had booked it. About 1 in 10 deletes has a reason.

      Deleted 24h+ aheadUsually plans changed

      Deleted 24 or more hours before the start. The official fill rate counts it as lost.

      Slots removedClient success

      The facility reduced the number of slots on a shift.

      Callout hoursNurse operations

      Hours of slots where a booked nurse called out. If another nurse worked the replacement, that replacement is a re-posted slot.

      Repeat callersNurse operations

      Approved callouts in the last 8 weeks, counted per nurse. Counts only. The dashboard shows no nurse names.

      No-showNurse operations

      A booked nurse didn't show up and didn't call.

      Facility-cancelled hoursClient success

      Hours of slots where the facility cancelled a booked nurse.

      Invalid timecardsClient success

      A nurse worked, but the timecard was marked invalid, so the hours don't count.

      Worked shorter than scheduledData / payroll

      Scheduled hours missing from timecards on worked slots: late clock-ins, early leaves, and unpaid breaks counted in scheduled hours.

      Hours postedLast 8 weeks

      Every slot-hour facilities posted.

      Includes shifts fully re-posted as new slots , which the fill rate leaves out so re-posted hours aren't counted twice. Shown in grey on the flow.

      Reached a booked nurse

      Posted hours a nurse was booked on and still held at the start, worked or not.

      Smaller than confirmed hours, which count every confirmed application, including ones later cancelled, called out or re-posted.

      Re-posted to replace

      Replacement slots after a callout or cancellation that another nurse worked. They count as new posted hours, as in the official fill rate.

      The numbers

      Fill rateLast 8 weeks

      Hours nurses worked ÷ slot-hours posted.

      Worked hours ÷ scheduled slot-hours, leaving out shifts fully re-posted as new slots. Shifts that started 2 to 58 days ago. Re-posted replacement slots and slots deleted 24h+ ahead count in posted hours.

      Expected fillLast 8 weeks

      What a market's or facility's shifts should fill, given how they were posted. A benchmark, not a cause.

      A logistic model on each shift's traits only: pay vs the local median for its license and state, posting lead time, weekend, time of day, slots, license, length, instant-pay, subscription billing, holiday, approval required, new facility. Nurse supply and reviews are left out. Fitted on the last 8 weeks against the official fill rate; checked on later weeks it never saw, predictions were within about 4 points in every tenth. Refit monthly.

      Fill vs expectedLast 8 weeks

      Fill rate minus expected fill, in points.

      Well below (−8 or worse): the market can't fill shifts that should fill, so it needs nurses or fewer callouts. Well above (+8 or more): spare capacity. Low expected fill: the shifts are hard to fill, which Client success can fix.

      Nurses shortLast 8 weeks

      Nurses needed to bring a Recruit nurses market up to its expected fill.

      Hours a week below expected fill, by license ÷ weekly hours per nurse. The low estimate divides by the hours a new nurse works in the first 90 days or a typical nurse here, whichever is more; the high estimate by whichever is less.

      Nurses working / weekLast 4 weeks

      Nurses who worked at least one shift that week, averaged over the last 4 full weeks.

      Compared with the same 4-week average six months earlier. New nurse users: within 60 days of becoming a lead.

      State colorsLast 8 weeks

      Short of nurses: 5+ nurses short in the state's Recruit nurses markets. More nurses than shifts: half or more of posted hours are in Spare capacity markets. Enough nurses: everything else.

      A state can be "Enough nurses" overall and still have a short metro area. Check Biggest nurse gaps.

      PipelineLast 30 to 150 days

      Sign-ups, approvals and first shifts in the last 30 days for nurses living in the metro area. Days to approve is the median for nurses approved in the last 90 days.

      Approval rate: share of sign-ups from 60 to 150 days ago approved within 60 days. Shown when there are 20+ sign-ups.

      Demand steadinessLast 26 weeks

      How much weekly posted hours swing.

      Steady: weekly posted hours vary under 15% around their average. Variable: 15–30%. Volatile: over 30%. New: under 12 weeks of history.

      ForecastNext 8 weeks

      Expected weekly posted hours if the recent trend continues.

      A straight-line trend through the last 25 full weeks, projected 8 weeks ahead, with a range that covers about 8 in 10 weeks. Nurses short in 8 weeks adds the share of new hours the market would likely miss.

      Likely to returnNext 90 days

      Lapsed nurses who will probably come back without a campaign.

      Nurses who worked in the area in the last 12 months but not anywhere in the last 90 days, × the share who came back on their own last time (now —). A good win-back campaign should beat this.

      Why the time windows differ

      Last 4 weeks
      The overview's headline numbers. Short enough to show today's level, compared with the same 4 weeks six months earlier.
      Last 8 weeks
      Long enough for a steady fill rate, expected fill and shortage, recent enough to reflect today. The last 2 days are left out while timecards come in.
      Last 26 weeks
      Enough history to tell a steady market from a swinging one.
      Next 8 weeks
      Roughly how long a new recruit takes to start working, so the forecast covers when they'd arrive.
      Last 90 days
      Nurses pick up shifts on and off. 90 days separates working from lapsed.
      Last 60 days
      The new facility window.

      Before acting on these numbers

      • Only about 1 in 10 deleted shifts has a reason. Some "deleted late" shifts may have been covered by the facility's own staff.
      • A few facilities can drive a market's numbers. Check with the market's client success manager before moving a large budget.
      • The forecast is a trend line, not a promise. Expected fill is refit monthly; it describes typical fill for shifts like these, not a promise either. Weigh it with what sales knows about upcoming contracts.
